# 0052: HTTP/1.1 requests are cut into TLS records as the browser cuts them

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-01
- Commits: (this commit)

**Summary.** An HTTP/1.1 request with a body now goes out in full 16 KiB
TLS records, its head apart from its body or not as the browser does
(profile `http1.uploadRecords`, generated from captures), and uTLS no
longer starts a connection with small records. mimicry used to send
every 4 KiB of a body as a record of its own, unlike every browser and
about half as fast. Every built-in profile has the field.

## Problem

`internal/h1` copied a body through a 4 KiB `bufio.Writer` straight onto
the TLS connection: a record and a `write` per 4 KiB, and through uTLS's
dynamic record sizing about 1.2 KB records for a connection's first
128 KB. Record sizes are visible on the path without decryption. Nothing
decided it: it came with the HTTP/1.1 client (issue #33). It also made
HTTP/1.1 uploads the slowest of the benchmarked clients
([performance](../performance.md)).

## Options

- **One cut for every browser** (16 KiB records, head and body one
  stream): what a TLS stack does with large writes. The captures below
  show three cuts, so it is wrong for Chrome and Safari.
- **A profile field, `http1.uploadRecords`, generated by hellogen from
  an `http1-upload` capture**: the head apart or not, and the largest
  request Chrome sends whole. Chosen.
- **Also modelling Chrome's Blob and File bodies** (never joined with
  the head): mimicry cannot tell a Blob from bytes; pinned (EVIDENCE.md,
  Not covered).

## Decision

- The capture server keeps every TLS record a client sends in scenarios
  with `recordUploads` (`server.recordLog.all`) and records per HTTP/1.1
  request how they carried it (`capture.H1Upload`: offset, head and body
  lengths, runs of record sizes with arrival times); those traces are
  gzipped (`.h1trace.json.gz`, `.h2trace.json.gz`). `http1-upload`
  uploads 10 MiB by `fetch()` from bytes (on a new connection, then on
  the same one), a Blob and FormData with a File, bodies from 1 byte to
  64 KiB (1-byte steps from 740 to 880, across Chrome's limit), small
  URLSearchParams and FormData bodies, a ReadableStream body and a
  10 MiB form POST. `h2-upload` uploads 10 MiB twice over HTTP/2.
- `capture.UploadRecords` classifies each request (`H1Upload.Cut`: one
  record, head apart, or one stream; every record full but the head's or
  body's last, or it fails) and derives `{headApart, joinUpTo}`; samples
  that disagree, or a join limit only bracketed, fail. Blob and File
  bodies count for `headApart` only. A version no longer installable as
  the brand takes it from its testing build, as in 0042.
- `internal/h1` writes each request through a pooled 16 KiB
  `bufio.Writer` (none held by an idle connection) over the connection's
  `Write` alone: each full buffer is one write and one record. With
  `headApart` the head is flushed alone unless head and body fit
  `joinUpTo`. A chunked body (never a browser's over HTTP/1.1) and a
  profile without the field (none built in) send one stream.
- `internal/handshake` sets `DynamicRecordSizingDisabled`: full records
  from a connection's first byte, over HTTP/1.1 and HTTP/2 alike.
- The uTLS fork moves to the commit whose `UConn.Write` no longer defers
  inside its CAS loop (a heap allocation per write, which the 4 KiB
  writes made 2,560 per 10 MiB); crypto/tls has the defer after the loop.

## Evidence

`http1-upload` and `h2-upload`, 3 samples each, 2026-11-01 (this Mac;
Chrome 156 on a Windows 10 PC; Safari 26.2 on the second Mac kept on
macOS 26; Safari 27.2 and 26 on two iPhones; Chrome for Testing
155.0.8059.84 for chrome-155), every sample agreeing:

| Browser | Head | Join | Body records | Last |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chrome 155, 156 (macOS, Windows) | alone | head+body ≤ 1400 B in one record (1400 joined, 1401 apart; Blob/File bodies never) | 16384 | remainder |
| Firefox 158 | one stream with the body | up to 16384 B in one record | 16384 from the head's first byte | remainder |
| Safari 26, 27 (macOS, iOS) | alone, from a 1-byte body | never | 16384 | remainder |

No browser grows its records: the first upload on a new connection
starts at 16384, as does `h2-upload`'s, which begins under 1 KB into
the connection (Chrome and Safari 16384 from the first DATA; Firefox's
follow its 16000-byte DATA frames). Over HTTP/1.1 no browser sent a
ReadableStream body chunked: Chrome and Safari refused it, Firefox sent
the string `[object ReadableStream]`.

`TestUploadRecordsMatchBrowser` compares mimicry's cut per request with
the rule the captures give; it fails with the 4 KiB writer, with the
head never flushed (Chrome, Safari) and with dynamic record sizing on.

A local benchmark (POST to a loopback Go server in the same process, M3
Pro), allocations counting the server's: 10 MiB 1,148 → 2,153 MB/s and
2,667 → 747 allocations per upload with this cut alone, 106 with the uTLS
fix too (2,133 MB/s; flat with body size); 64 KiB 666 → 863 MB/s, 123 →
106 allocations.

The Linux benchmark (Linux host (Intel Core i5-13500), 3 passes,
[report](../../internal/bench/reports/linux-uploads-2026-11-01/uploads-2026-11-01.md)
against the [run before](../../internal/bench/reports/linux-uploads-2026-10-31/uploads-2026-10-31.md)),
HTTP/1.1 one at a time where the server was not the limit: 64 KB 496 →
846 MB/s to nginx and 496 → 826 MB/s to the Go server, 134 → 73 µs of
CPU per upload; 10 MB to the Go server 715 → 2,001 MB/s, 14.3 → 5.1 ms,
2,648 → 86 allocations. mimicry went from the slowest client at these
points to the fastest at 64 KB, and to 3% behind httpcloak at 10 MB.
HTTP/2 uploads to nginx at 10 MB went from about 900 to 96 allocations.

## Consequences

HTTP/1.1 uploads look like the browser's on the wire and cost a quarter
of the writes. Each request takes a 16 KiB buffer from a pool while it is
written; an idle connection no longer holds 4 KiB. HTTP/2's first 128 KB
are no longer cut small either.

## Revisit when

A capture shows a browser cutting records by timing or flow, or a
browser streaming request bodies over HTTP/1.1.
