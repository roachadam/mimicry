# HTTP/3 when QUIC fails, QUIC version 2, and a long transfer: Chrome 155, Firefox 158, Safari 27 and Safari 26, 2026-10-25

Five scenarios (`internal/server/h3edge.go`), three samples per browser and
condition, all in `harness/testdata/captures/`:

| Scenario | Server | Captures |
|---|---|---|
| `h3-blackhole` | Alt-Svc names a UDP port that records every datagram and answers none | `<name>.h3-blackhole` (all four, unforced: no certificate is ever sent, so `-h3-local-roots` changes nothing) |
| `h3-broken` | HTTP/3 works on `h3-a` and `h3-b`; (a) `silence.so` goes unanswered and its connection's 4-tuple goes dark both ways; (b) `close.cs` is answered, then GOAWAY + CONNECTION_CLOSE, and no QUIC connection started after that is answered | Chrome, Firefox `.local-roots`; Safari 27, 26 unforced |
| `h3-v2` | aioquic 1.7 (Safari 26's Mac: 1.2) behind the capture socket, offering v1 and v2, upgrading compatibly (RFC 9368) | Chrome, Firefox `.local-roots`; Safari 27, 26 |
| `h3-vn` | quic-go with version 2 only: Version Negotiation for v1 | Chrome, Firefox `.local-roots`; Safari 27, 26 |
| `h3-download` | quic-go, one 50 MiB incompressible response, then 3 small requests | Chrome, Firefox `.local-roots`; Safari 27, 26 |

Every step starts on a fixed schedule (`NoWait`) and its URL carries `?t=`,
the script's `performance.now()`. **Delay** below is a request's arrival at
the server minus its `t`, less the smallest such difference in the sample
(the page's clock offset; that request had no wait). So a delay is the time
the browser held the request before it reached the server, its new
connection's handshake included. Times are page ms (since the server started
serving the page) unless given as "+x", which is from the event named. The
recorder now also keeps the server's datagrams (`server`), the server's
transport parameters, and connections a client moves to another port
(`path`).

Unless a count is given, a finding holds in 3 of 3 samples.

**Corrections** (from the captures, when the profile generators were made to
read them; the first version of this report said otherwise):

- **Chrome closes at its give-up.** Every unanswered attempt (3 of 3
  blackhole samples, both attempts of each h3-broken sample) ends at +4.0 s
  with an Initial CONNECTION_CLOSE, NO_ERROR, reason
  `25:No recent network activity after {µs}us. Timeout:4s
  num_undecryptable_packets: 0 {}`, padded to 1250 bytes; a resumed
  attempt's datagram carries a 0-RTT packet after it (not opened: the
  recorder had no 0-RTT keys for it). Not "no CONNECTION_CLOSE".
- **Firefox's resumed give-up is a close.** A fresh attempt ends silently
  (blackhole); the resumed re-validation in h3-broken is closed by necko at
  +5.0 s (2 samples; +9.2 s in the third, which used no cached RTT) with an
  Initial CONNECTION_CLOSE, APPLICATION_ERROR (0x0c) without a reason (RFC
  9000, section 10.2.3), a 0-RTT packet after it, in a 255-byte datagram.
- **Firefox's retry after Version Negotiation sends another ClientHello**:
  512 bytes, without X25519MLKEM768 (groups x25519, P-256, P-384, P-521; an
  x25519 key share only), compress_certificate or the ECH GREASE extension,
  and with a padding extension last (`tls.quic.version_negotiation`).
- **Chrome has a second dead-path rule.** On the migrated path of h3-broken
  (a), its keep-alive PINGs (from +15 s idle) went unanswered; after 6.4 s of
  backed-off PINGs it closed the connection with `85:Network blackhole
  detected` and sent the stuck `silence.so` again on a new TCP connection.
  That is the resend §3 (a) found 54.4–61.4 s after the request.

## 2. Blackhole (`h3-blackhole`)

Steps (fetch, 1 s apart): `fetch1`, `fetch2`, `goaway1` (HTTP/2 GOAWAY),
`after-goaway1` (+1.5 s), `fetch3` (+1), `fetch4` (+3), `fetch5` (+5),
`goaway2` (+10), `after-goaway2` (+1.5), `fetch6` (+5), `last` (+20).

- **Chrome** (`chrome-155-macos.h3-blackhole`): opens two TCP connections at
  the start (the second a preconnect), triggers QUIC with `fetch1.so`
  (QUIC's first Initial in the same ms as the request arrived over HTTP/2).
  The requests that had a connection never waited. `after-goaway1.so`, the
  first request after the server's GOAWAY, came 3.5 s into the QUIC attempt
  and waited 305–306 ms before going on the preconnected connection. At
  `goaway2` (25 s) Chrome opened a new TCP connection (resumed) with no QUIC
  datagram beside it, delay 4 ms: the alternative was broken. The
  retransmissions are 1250-byte single Initials carrying a different subset
  of the ClientHello's CRYPTO data each time, as the first flight (its
  chaos-protection layout); the packet number skips one per PTO (1, 2, 4, 6,
  8, then 9).
- **Firefox** (`firefox-158-macos.h3-blackhole`): QUIC starts with `/`'s
  Alt-Svc and runs beside HTTP/2; its requests never wait. The PTO doubles
  from 300 ms (300, 605, 1210, ~2500, ~5000, ~10000 ms intervals), each time
  both Initial packets again (same CRYPTO split, new packet numbers). The
  last flight is at +19.0–19.8 s; no CONNECTION_CLOSE was sent (none could
  be: no keys beyond Initial, and Firefox sent no Initial close either).
  Firefox's own GOAWAY follows each server GOAWAY, as on every page.
- **Safari 27/26** (`safari-27-macos`, `safari-26-macos.h3-blackhole`): no
  QUIC until a request needs a new connection; then two Initials, and TCP
  ~100 ms later (89–129 ms over 12 attempts). No QUIC retransmission at
  all: the TCP connection won before Safari's first PTO. The same happens
  at the second GOAWAY: no broken-marking within the page.

## 3. Broken after use (`h3-broken`)

Steps: `warm.cs`, `goaway.so`/`goaway.cs` (HTTP/2 GOAWAY only), `a1`, `b1`,
`a2`, `b2`; (a) `silence.so`, then `silent1–6.so` at +1, +3, +6, +11, +21,
+33 s with `keep1.cs` (+8) and `keep2.cs` (+23) on `h3-b`; (b) `close.cs`,
then `closed1–6.cs` at +2, +4, +7, +12, +20, +30 s. The server silences only
the first `silence.so` (a repeat on another connection is answered).

### (a) Silence

- **Chrome** (`chrome-155-macos.h3-broken.local-roots`): two
  retransmissions of the request on the old port, then at +115–131 ms a
  1250-byte PATH_CHALLENGE from a new port, PING and RETIRE_CONNECTION_ID,
  the request again at +240 ms, PATH_RESPONSE to the server's challenge,
  and every later request on the new path (`path` 1 in the capture) with no
  delay. The silenced request itself was never answered by the server
  (it drops the request, a scenario artefact); Chrome sent it again over a
  new TCP connection 54.4–61.4 s later, when it closed the migrated
  connection with `85:Network blackhole detected` after its keep-alive
  PINGs there went unanswered (*corrected*: this said why was not
  captured).
- **Firefox** (`firefox-158-macos.h3-broken.local-roots`): no migration.
  The connection's PTO probes back off (table); new requests go on the dead
  connection. At the 30 s idle timeout (server's view and Firefox's TCP
  agree within 9 ms: 38.14–38.39 s) Firefox opens 6 TCP connections and
  sends all six pending requests on one. `silent6.so`, 3 s later, goes TCP
  at once with a new QUIC connection (fresh, not resumed) beside it, which
  completes and carries no request (Alt-Svc validation again).
- **Safari 27/26** (`safari-27-macos`, `safari-26-macos.h3-broken`): no
  migration and no TCP. Retransmissions back off from ~30 ms; PINGs about
  every second; new requests still sent on the dead connection. At +12.0 s
  (12,006–12,015 ms) Safari sends CONNECTION_CLOSE (NO_ERROR) on it and
  8–16 ms later opens a resumed connection with every pending request in
  0-RTT (`silence.so` included), which the server answers.

### (b) Close, then nothing answered

The close: GOAWAY after `close.cs`'s response, CONNECTION_CLOSE H3_NO_ERROR
1 s later. Safari 27/26 closed the connection itself on the GOAWAY (the
server recorded a client close before its own).

- **Chrome**: `closed1.cs` starts a resumed connection with 0-RTT
  (16 datagrams over 4.0 s, nothing answered) and **no TCP**; at +4.0 s a
  fresh QUIC attempt and a TCP connection start in the same millisecond, and
  both pending requests go TCP (delays 4.0 s and 1.0–2.0 s). Nothing after
  tries QUIC (25 s left). This is the stall the interop report's bug 2
  describes: a 0-RTT attempt is not raced.
- **Firefox**: TCP first (7–8 ms before the resumed QUIC attempt), so
  `closed1.cs` is not delayed. But `closed2.cs` and `closed3.cs` were held
  until that QUIC attempt ended (+5.0 s in 2 samples, +9.2 s in 1): Firefox
  dispatched them to the pending HTTP/3 connection rather than to the open
  HTTP/2 one, then opened another TCP connection. `closed4.cs` (10 s after
  the first attempt) started a fresh QUIC attempt beside TCP; none after.
- **Safari**: QUIC first, TCP ~100 ms later, for `closed1.cs` (resumed:
  Initial + 0-RTT) and `closed2.cs` (fresh); from `closed3.cs` on, TCP
  only, no QUIC: two failures within the page stop it.

## 4. QUIC version 2

### Compatible upgrade (`h3-v2`, aioquic)

**Firefox** (`firefox-158-macos.h3-v2.local-roots`), every sample:

1. Client: Initial v1 ×2 (first flight; version_information chosen
   `00000001`, available [GREASE, `6b3343cf`, `00000001`], 16 bytes).
2. Server: Initial **v1** pn 0 (ACK + PING).
3. Client: Initial v1 (ACK + CRYPTO, padded to 1252).
4. Server: Initial **v2** pn 1 + Handshake v2 (coalesced), Handshake v2 +
   1-RTT, Initial v2 (ACK). Server version_information: chosen
   `6b3343cf`, available [`00000001`, `6b3343cf`].
5. Client: Initial **v2** (ACK only) + Handshake v2 (ACK), padded to 1252;
   then Handshake v2 (Finished) + 1-RTT. The Initial packet number space
   runs on across the switch (v1 pn 19 → v2 pn 20). Initial keys for v2
   come from the client's original Destination Connection ID (the recorder
   opens them so); 1-RTT uses the `quicv2` labels.
6. `negotiated_version` `6b3343cf` on the connection; its requests on
   streams 0–16.
7. 35 s later (after the 30 s idle timeout), the next connection is
   **resumed and starts in v2**: Initial v2 ×2 (second coalesced with
   0-RTT v2), a 0-RTT v2 datagram, no v1 packet at all; version_information
   chosen `6b3343cf`, available unchanged (fresh GREASE). Token length 0:
   aioquic sends no NEW_TOKEN, so whether Firefox would present a v2
   connection's token is not measured.

**Chrome** (`chrome-155-macos.h3-v2.local-roots`): v1 only (its
version_information lists 1 and GREASE); 0-RTT resumed v1 after idle.
**Safari 27/26** (`safari-27-macos`, `safari-26-macos.h3-v2`): v1 only, no
version_information sent; aioquic still sends its own (chosen 1).

### Incompatible negotiation (`h3-vn`, quic-go v2 only)

The server answers each Initial datagram with its own VN packet listing
`6b3343cf` and a reserved version, in random order.

- **Firefox** (`firefox-158-macos.h3-vn.local-roots`): a new connection
  1 ms later in v2 (Initial v2, version_information chosen `6b3343cf`). The
  server's Initial v2 + Handshake v2 arrive; Firefox answers with Initial +
  Handshake ACKs, then CONNECTION_CLOSE (0x1c) error **0x53f8** in Handshake
  and 1-RTT packets, because the server's transport parameters carry no
  version_information (RFC 9368 downgrade check; 0x53f8 is the draft's
  VERSION_NEGOTIATION_ERROR, not RFC 9368's 0x11). No further QUIC; TCP.
- **Chrome**, **Safari 27/26**: no v2 attempt. Chrome had its HTTP/2
  connections open, so no request waited; Safari's request waited for TCP
  as after a blackhole (table).

## 5. Long transfer (`h3-download`)

Steps: `fetch1`, `goaway.so` (HTTP/2 GOAWAY), `fetch2`, `download.so`
(50 MiB, waited for), then `after1–3.so` 1 s apart. quic-go starts a key
update after 100 packets and not again before 100,000, so any later flip
would be the browser's: none came. Its server 1-RTT headers are unmasked
(key phase, packet number) from its key log.

The credit rules (table) read: the browser sends MAX_STREAM_DATA once a
fixed share of its window has been consumed, setting the limit to bytes
consumed + the initial window. The window never grew during 50 MiB
(Chrome 6 MiB, update every half; Firefox 12 MiB, every quarter; Safari
2 MiB, about every half). Connection credit: Chrome 15 MiB, every half;
Firefox 24 MiB, every quarter; Safari tracks the stream limit. Chrome also
repeats its current MAX_DATA in the packet after every 19 ACK-only ones,
the rule `quic.ack_only_limit` already models. ACK delay fields are mostly
raw 0–5 (×8 µs); ACK frames carry 1–4 ranges (gaps: [INFERENCE] loss on
the loopback socket at about 200 MB/s).

## 6. Recorder and scenario notes

- `quictrace` decodes v2 (salt, `quicv2` labels, type bits, Retry integrity
  tag), keeps Initial keys per version from the original DCID, records the
  server's datagrams (Initial/Handshake opened, VN versions, Retry checked,
  1-RTT header unmasked), the server's transport parameters, and a
  connection's datagrams from a new client port (Chrome's migration). The
  committed v1 captures load unchanged (no stored field moved).
- `hellogen` and `h3gen` now also read the edge captures
  (`server.H3EdgeCaptures`: all but `h3-v2`) for the QUIC failure, version
  and transfer fields (*corrected*: this said the generators read only the
  `h3-discovery` and `h3-page` captures); `kindsgen` still reads only those.
- A 0-RTT connection whose handshake the client abandoned keeps `h3` nil
  (`Rederive`), even when the server answered a request it carried in
  0-RTT (Firefox's first `h3-broken` take, not committed).
- aioquic's HTTP/3 is aioquic's, so `h3-v2` connections have no `h3`
  record; their request streams show as STREAM frames.
- The `h3-blackhole`, `h3-v2` and `h3-vn` captures were taken before the
  recorder followed new client ports and unmasked server 1-RTT headers
  (no capture of theirs needs either: no migration, no long transfer);
  `h3-broken` and `h3-download` were taken with both.

## 7. What a model needs

1. **v2, compatible (Firefox only):** accept a server's Initial in v2 after
   a v1 first flight (keys from the original DCID, `quicv2` labels and
   type bits), keep acknowledging v1 Initials received before it in v1, then
   send every long header in v2; Initial packet numbers continue. Remember
   v2 for the origin: the resumed connection starts in v2 (chosen
   `6b3343cf` in version_information, available order unchanged) with 0-RTT
   in v2. Chrome and Safari never leave v1.
2. **VN:** Firefox retries at once in v2, with its other ClientHello
   (*corrected*, §1), and aborts with 0x53f8 if the server's transport
   parameters lack version_information; Chrome and Safari go to TCP.
3. **No answer to a new connection:** Chrome: PTO 300 ms, 1250-byte single
   Initials, give up at +4.0 s with a padded Initial CONNECTION_CLOSE
   (*corrected*, §1); race TCP at once when no
   0-RTT is possible, or wait 300 ms when a TCP connection exists;
   **with 0-RTT, wait the whole 4.0 s, then start a fresh QUIC attempt and
   TCP together**; mark the alternative broken for the rest of the page
   after a failed fresh attempt. Firefox: never put a request behind an
   unvalidated QUIC connection, except requests made while a re-validation
   is pending after a close (held until it fails, 5–9 s); PTO 300 ms
   doubling, give-up ~19 s, silent (first attempt), or ~5–9 s with an
   Initial application close in 255 bytes (resumed ones; *corrected*, §1); retry
   validation on the next request ~3–10 s later. Safari: QUIC first, TCP
   after 100 ms, abandon QUIC when TCP connects; no broken-marking until two
   failures in a row after use.
4. **Path dies mid-connection:** Chrome migrates to a new port after
   ~120 ms (4 of its PTOs) and continues, and closes with `85:Network
   blackhole detected`, the stuck request to TCP, when the new path dies
   too (*corrected*, §1); Firefox waits for its 30 s idle timeout, then TCP;
   Safari closes after 12 s and reconnects with 0-RTT, replaying pending
   requests.
5. **Long transfers:** no browser starts a key update (only follow); credit
   as in the table, fixed windows; ACK every 10 packets for Chrome, every
   2–4 for Safari, Firefox irregular; no ACK_FREQUENCY frames.

Not measured: Retry from a browser's view, a NEW_TOKEN from a v2 connection
used by a v1 one (aioquic sends none), broken-alternative durations beyond
a page (Chrome's 5 minutes is source only), what Safari does after more
than two failures, migration by Firefox or Safari (none seen), key updates
beyond 37,000 received packets, and credit growth beyond 50 MiB or at
lower bandwidth.
