# HTTP/3 in Chrome 155, Firefox 158, Safari 27 and Safari 26, 2026-10-25

The `h3-discovery` and `h3-page` scenarios (`server.H3DiscoverySteps`,
`H3PageSteps`; decision 0026). Every response over TCP and QUIC carries
`Alt-Svc: h3=":<port>"; ma=86400`. `h3-discovery` allows no QPACK dynamic
table; `h3-page` allows 64 KiB and 100 blocked streams. Three samples per
browser and condition:

- unforced: `<name>.h3-{discovery,page}`, all four browsers;
- `-h3-local-roots`: `<name>.h3-{discovery,page}.local-roots`, Chrome
  (`--origin-to-force-quic-on=www.h3-a.test:1,www.h3-b.test:1`) and Firefox
  (`network.http.http3.disable_when_third_party_roots_found=false`), each
  recorded in `Page.Conditions`.

Safari 26 comes from the second Mac. Unless a count is given, a finding holds
in every sample of the scenario and condition named. Times are on the page
clock (ms since the server started serving the page) unless they are given
as "+x" from a connection's first datagram. ACK delays are the raw field ×8
µs: no browser sends `ack_delay_exponent`, so it is the default 3. `h3-a` is
`www.h3-a.test` (the page's site) and `h3-b` is `www.h3-b.test` (the other
site).

## 2. Safari: why "no requests" (diagnosis)

**The premise does not hold for the committed captures (46771b9).** Every
Safari `h3-discovery` sample, 3 of Safari 27 and 3 of Safari 26, has one QUIC
connection (to `h3-a`, 14–15 datagrams, no close). Its `H3.Requests` has 3
request streams, each with a complete field section and FIN:

| Stream | Path | When |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | `/h3/after-goaway.so` | +5–10 ms after the connection's first datagram |
| 4 | `/h3/after-idle.so` | 35.0 s later |
| 8 | `/done` | 8 ms after that |

Checked against the raw frames:

- **Decryption:** every packet of every Safari datagram decrypted (no
  `error` on any packet anywhere in these captures). The 1-RTT frames:
  - pn 0: `STREAM(s2 0+21)`, the control stream;
  - pn 1: `ACK` plus `STREAM(s0 0+266 FIN)`, a 263-byte HEADERS frame plus
    3 bytes of framing;
  - then ACK-only packets, `STREAM(s4 0+264 FIN)` 35 s later, and
    `STREAM(s8 …FIN)`.

  Every STREAM frame on the wire has a recorded stream (a cross-check of all
  captures found no unrecorded stream and no recorded stream without frames).
- **Streams:** control stream 2 only: SETTINGS `QPACK_MAX_TABLE_CAPACITY`
  16383, `QPACK_BLOCKED_STREAMS` 100, one GREASE setting. No QPACK encoder
  or decoder stream; the server offered no table in this scenario.
- **Why it connected so late:** the TCP side has no copies of these
  requests. Safari sent every request before the GOAWAY on its HTTP/2
  connections, although every response advertised h3. It moved `h3-a` to
  QUIC only when that connection was gone (the server's GOAWAY after
  `goaway.so`): `after-goaway.so`, 2 s later, opened the QUIC connection
  with no TCP handshake beside it. `h3-b`'s HTTP/2 connection was never
  closed, so `xafter-idle.cs` went on it 35 s later. In `h3-page`, where no
  connection closes, Safari sent no UDP datagram at all.
- **Why no close:** the page ends when `/done` is answered, about 50.4–51 s
  in, and the connection was still open then. Safari 27 advertises
  `max_idle_timeout` 300000 and Safari 26 none, so the server's 60 s
  applies. Safari sent no PING in the 35 s gap and still reused the
  connection.

**Not a recorder bug.** Two things may have looked like "no requests": the
connection has only one unidirectional stream, and the `h3-page` captures
have no `quic` key at all.

Recorder issues that these captures do show (none affects Safari's data):

1. **Transport parameter `0xff02de1a` is mislabeled GREASE.** This is
   `min_ack_delay` in the ack-frequency draft-01/02 codepoint, which Firefox
   sends with value 1000 µs (hex `43e8`). It happens to be 27 mod 31, so
   `IsGreaseTransportParameter` flags it, and its value is not decoded. Fix in
   `internal/harness/internal/quictrace/tparams.go`:
   - add `0xff02de1a: "min_ack_delay"` to `transportParameterNames` and
     `0xff02de1a: true` to `varintParameters`;
   - in `ParseTransportParameters`, set
     `Grease: transportParameterNames[id] == "" && IsGreaseTransportParameter(id)`,
     so no known id is ever GREASE;
   - add a `tparams_test.go` case with Firefox's body.

   The committed captures keep `client_hello` hex, so re-parsing fixes them
   without recapturing.
2. **`H3.Streams` and `H3.Requests` are in the server's accept/read order,
   not wire order.** Firefox's three unidirectional streams travel in one
   packet as `STREAM(s2) STREAM(s6) STREAM(s10)` but are recorded as
   10, 6, 2 or 10, 2, 6. Chrome's and Firefox's markup requests are
   recorded out of stream order (for example s24 before s8). Fix: in
   `pageLog.quicConns` (`internal/harness/internal/server/quic.go`), after
   `match.H3, match.Close = h.snapshot()`, stable-sort both slices by the
   wire position of their stream's first STREAM frame in `match.Datagrams`
   (datagram index, packet index, frame index). Do it with a helper in
   `quictrace`, falling back to the stream ID. Alternatively, change the
   field comments to say "in the order the server accepted them".
3. **`H3Request.Blocked` reflects server goroutine timing, not the browser.**
   Chrome sends its encoder-stream inserts before the HEADERS that refer to
   them, in the same packet (for example `STREAM(s10 570+88)
   STREAM(s12 0+20 FIN)`), yet 5 of its 72 `h3-page` requests are flagged
   blocked, because the request stream was decoded before the encoder
   stream's goroutine applied the inserts (`decodeSection` in
   `server/http3.go`). Fix: record each encoder instruction's end offset in
   the encoder stream. After decoding, mark a request blocked only if its
   Required Insert Count exceeds the number of instructions that end at or
   before the highest encoder-stream offset sent in STREAM frames that
   precede the request's first HEADERS STREAM frame in wire order.
   Otherwise document `Blocked` as "the server waited", not "the client
   risked blocking".
4. **Minor:** `Conn.H3` is documented as nil when the handshake never
   completed, but the unforced Chrome and Firefox connections carry
   `{"streams":[],"requests":[]}`. The server accepts early, so an `h3conn`
   exists. Either change the comment, or leave `H3` nil when the handshake
   did not complete.

### After the server's first flight

Fresh connections, datagram by datagram:

- **Chrome:**
  1. Handshake (ACK, CRYPTO Finished 36 B) coalesced with a 1-RTT packet
     carrying the control stream (SETTINGS and a GREASE frame, 39–46 B);
     146–153 B in all.
  2. A 1-RTT ACK.

  Chrome does **not ACK the server's Initial** (it did in 1 of 12 completed
  fresh handshakes).
- **Firefox:**
  1. Initial ACK + Handshake ACK, padded to 1252 with trailing zeros (in 2
     of 12 unforced samples they were two separate datagrams).
  2. Handshake CRYPTO Finished + 1-RTT (ACK and NEW_CONNECTION_ID seq 1–3),
     176 B.
  3. 1-RTT with `STREAM(s2) STREAM(s6) STREAM(s10)` (control, encoder,
     decoder).
  4. NEW_CONNECTION_ID seq 4, 0.3–1.5 ms later.
- **Safari:**
  1. Initial ACK padded to 1200 with PADDING frames.
  2. Handshake ACK alone (39 B).
  3. Handshake CRYPTO Finished alone (73 B).
  4. 1-RTT control stream alone (50 B).
  5. 1-RTT ACK + the request.

  Nothing is coalesced.

1-RTT header bits:

- **Spin bit:** Chrome always 0. Firefox varies within 18 of 21 connections, in no
  stable pattern (quic-go never spins, so a spinning client would hold
  steady at 1). Safari 0 in its first 1-RTT packet, then always 1, which
  is what a spinning client shows against quic-go.
- **Key phase:** Chrome and Firefox flip it once, at pn 59–72, in their
  long `h3-page` connection. quic-go starts a key update after 100 packets
  (`FirstKeyUpdateInterval`), so the browsers followed the server; neither
  started one itself.
- **1-RTT packet numbers:** 1 byte, except Chrome's from about 138 on.

Largest datagrams seen: Chrome 1250, Firefox 1252, Safari 1200, including
the 64 KiB upload.

### How connections end

- **Idle:** Chrome and Firefox go silent. The server's idle timer fired
  exactly 30.0 s after their last datagram (the minimum of their 30 s and
  the server's 60 s), with no PING and no CONNECTION_CLOSE. Safari's
  connection outlived the 35 s gap with no PING, because its advertised
  idle timeout is 300 s or none.
- **Server GOAWAY, then CONNECTION_CLOSE H3_NO_ERROR 1 s later:** neither
  Chrome nor Firefox closed or sent anything on the control stream after the
  GOAWAY.
  - Chrome ACKed and then sent nothing; the server's close got no reply.
  - Firefox answered the server's CONNECTION_CLOSE at once with its own:
    **transport `CONNECTION_CLOSE` 0x1c, error 0 (NO_ERROR), frame type
    0x1d**, in the same packet as an ACK (3 of 3).
- **Page end:** nobody closes; the connections were open when `/done` was
  answered.

### 0-RTT on later connections

Every resumed QUIC connection of Chrome (9) and Firefox (9) sent 0-RTT data;
the server accepts it:

- **Chrome:** two Initial datagrams (1250 B, with the token), then two
  separate, unpadded 0-RTT datagrams:
  1. the control stream (SETTINGS, GREASE frame), 75–82 B;
  2. the PRIORITY_UPDATE and the request (`STREAM(s2) STREAM(s0 FIN)`),
     420–438 B.

  Then the Handshake Finished.
- **Firefox:** the third datagram coalesces the last Initial (CRYPTO tail)
  with a 0-RTT packet: NEW_CONNECTION_ID seq 1, then **the request stream
  first** (`STREAM(s0 FIN)`), then control, encoder and decoder. The
  datagram is padded to 1252 with trailing zeros. The next datagram
  coalesces Initial ACK (+PING in 3 of 9), Handshake (ACK, Finished) and
  1-RTT (ACK, NEW_CONNECTION_ID seq 2–3, PADDING to 1252).
- **Safari:** never resumed a QUIC connection (one per sample).

## 4. Discovery and racing (`-h3-local-roots` for Chrome and Firefox)

| | Chrome | Firefox | Safari 27, 26 (unforced) |
|---|---|---|---|
| When QUIC starts | on the **first request to an origin whose Alt-Svc is known**. ## 5. Summary: what mimicry must model

"Data" means a per-profile value; "code" means behaviour the engine must
implement. Values are as above; "same as h2" means the existing HTTP/2
profile data applies.

**Sample-variable** (model as random, not as fixed values):

- Chrome: the extension order, the transport parameter order, the GREASE
  transport parameter's id and length (0–15), the GREASE version value and
  its position, the ECH GREASE payload length (144–240), the CRYPTO
  fragmentation, PING/PADDING placement, the GREASE setting and frame ids
  and frame length, the token on the `h3-b` reconnection, and whether the
  preconnect socket is reused after GOAWAY.
- Firefox: the extension order (with fixed tail), the ECH AEAD (1 or 3), the
  initial DCID length, the initial packet number, the CRYPTO cut position
  (follows the SNI's position), the GREASE frame type and length, the spin
  bit, whether the Initial and Handshake ACKs share a datagram (10 of 12),
  PING with the resumed Initial ACK (3 of 9), and one STOP_SENDING (1 of 3).
- Safari: the transport parameter rotation and the constant ACK delay value.

**Unmeasured:**

- **Safari:**
  - QPACK with a dynamic table;
  - any request kind on HTTP/3 other than `fetch()`;
  - PRIORITY_UPDATE;
  - resumption and 0-RTT;
  - what it does when its HTTP/3 connection closes or idles out;
  - whether it ever moves a live HTTP/2 connection to HTTP/3.
- **All browsers:**
  - credit growth (MAX_DATA/MAX_STREAM_DATA/MAX_STREAMS under large
    downloads; responses here are tiny);
  - key updates started by the browser;
  - PMTU probing, ECN, migration, DATAGRAM;
  - discovery through DNS HTTPS records (the stub does not answer them);
  - Retry and Version Negotiation;
  - a server that uses the dynamic table in responses (decoder stream
    instructions);
  - the meaning of Firefox's transport parameter `0x1d` and Safari's
    `0xff080808`.
- **What `-h3-local-roots` might change:** the unforced first flights match
  the local-roots ones, but everything after the handshake was only seen
  with the setting:
  - Chrome: forcing QUIC on `:1` origins ([INFERENCE] from source: it only
    lifts the known-root check for those hosts; the switching and racing
    seen here may still differ with a built-in root);
  - Firefox: the pref only skips the third-party-root check ([INFERENCE]: no
    wire effect).
- **Server-shaped behaviour:** quic-go's server shaped some of what was
  seen: its NEW_TOKEN (the 90-byte tokens), its key update at 100 packets,
  its 8-byte CIDs, its 60 s idle timeout (Safari's connection survived 35 s
  because of it), and its accepting 0-RTT. A real server's ACK_FREQUENCY
  (Firefox advertises `min_ack_delay`) was not exercised.
