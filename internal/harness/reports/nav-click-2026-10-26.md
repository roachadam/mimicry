# Clicked and form-POST navigation redirects (2026-10-26)

Captures: `<name>.nav-redirect-click.pagetrace.json.gz` (Chrome 155,
Firefox 158, Safari 27; 49 steps) and `<name>.nav-redirect-form.pagetrace.json.gz`
(all four browsers; 40 steps), three samples each, every sample complete.
Safari 26 has no click capture (see below).

## What was captured

- `nav-redirect-click`: navigations with user activation. The capture tool
  clicks the page's link or submit button as a user does: Chrome over the
  DevTools protocol (`Input.dispatchMouseEvent`), Firefox over WebDriver
  BiDi (`input.performActions`), Safari through the system's mouse events
  (`CGEventPost`). Links answered 301/302 within an origin, to another
  origin of the page's site, to another site, from the apex to www, from
  another site within it, to its www, to the page's site and back to the
  page's origin; form POSTs answered 301, 302, 303, 307 and 308 for each of
  so, so→ss, so→cs, ss, ss(apex)→so, cs, cs→ss, cs→so.
- `nav-redirect-form`: the same form POSTs submitted by script.

Every Chrome and Firefox request of the click capture carries
`sec-fetch-user: ?1`, first legs and hops. Compared header names per
request (84 pairs per browser: each clicked form against its script twin,
clicked links against `nav-redirect-page`'s script navigations), a click is
the script's order plus `sec-fetch-user` (Chrome between `sec-fetch-mode`
and `sec-fetch-dest`, Firefox after `sec-fetch-site`); Safari 27's are
identical to its script's.

## Finding: `Origin: null` on 307/308 navigation hops

A form POST kept by a 307 or 308 sends `Origin: null` after a redirect to
another origin, by two rules:

| Hop | Chrome | Safari 27, 26 | Firefox |
|---|---|---|---|
| so, ss, cs (within an origin) | page origin | page origin | page origin |
| so→ss, so→cs | `null` | `null` | page origin |
| ss→so, cs→ss, cs→so | `null` | `null` | `null` |

Firefox follows the Fetch standard's tainted origin; Chrome and Safari null
it on any redirect to another origin. mimicry sent the page's origin on
all of them; the new `redirects.navigation_origin` (`cross_origin`,
`taint`) models both.

## Changes

- kindsgen merges clicked navigations with the script's of the same
  condition: every Chrome and Firefox navigation variant (but the
  non-secure one) now has a `sec-fetch-user` slot. Regenerated.
- `resolve`: a user's navigation whose variant has no `sec-fetch-user`
  slot is `ErrNotMeasured` (first legs too, not only hops); over HTTP/1.1
  where the HTTP/1.1 list has none (Firefox's form POST) the connection
  refuses it.
- `TestNavRedirectsMatchBrowser` replays both pages, a click with
  `Sec-Fetch-User`, a script's without.

Public API, before and after (HEAD 9f32f39 vs this change), a clicked
link `GET /link` → 302 → `/landed` from `/page`: before, Chrome and
Firefox returned `ErrNotMeasured` after the first request; now both send
`/link` and `/landed` with `sec-fetch-user: ?1`, `sec-fetch-site:
same-origin` and the page's Referer, as the captures show.

Mutation checks: Chrome's `navigation_origin` set to `taint` fails
`TestNavRedirectsMatchBrowser` (form and click pages); sending the clicks
as a script's (nil `Sec-Fetch-User`) fails it for Chrome's click page;
dropping the first-leg check fails `TestRefusedRequests`; dropping the
HTTP/1.1 slot check fails `TestHTTP2OnlyVariantOverHTTP1`.
