### `chainPriorities`

Each request depends exclusively on the newest stream still open at the nearest weight at or above its own (Chromium's lists). A stream leaves them at the peer's END_STREAM or a reset, whether or not its body was read.

- **Chrome:** W (every Chrome HTTP/2 trace, replays included).
  - 3006 of 11795 HEADERS of Chrome 155 and 156 depend on a stream, never one of lower weight.
  - Every connection fits the rule.
  - The newest open stream regardless of weight cannot fit 9 of Chrome 155's 1622 connections.
  - A stream is still a parent after 3 MB of its response arrived (`replay-nike.com`), so not at its response headers.
  - None is once the page has the whole response.
  - That the moment is its END_STREAM, not the body's reading, is Chromium's code, `SpdyStream::OnDataReceived`: [INFERENCE].
- **Firefox:** W: never (stream 0, not exclusive).
- **Safari 27 / 26:** W: never (no priority).
- **Checked by:**
  - `TestChainedPrioritiesMatchBrowserTraces`: every trace's requests sent again, each stream ended after the last that depends on it, half with their bodies unread.
  - `TestEndedStreamLeavesChainBeforeDelivery` (unit: a stream leaves the chain before its end reaches the caller).
