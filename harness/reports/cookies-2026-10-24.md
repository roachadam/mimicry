# Cookies in Chrome 155, Firefox 158 and Safari 27, 2026-10-24

Which cookies each browser accepted, and which it sent on every request of
the kinds page (`kinds` and `kinds-http1` captures, 3 samples each, stable
unless noted). Input for `mimicry/cookies` (step 9.3).

## Third-party cookies (set by `cdn.kinds-b.test` on an image)

| Cookie | Chrome | Firefox | Safari |
|---|---|---|---|
| `kb_none` (`SameSite=None`) | sent cross-site | sent | **blocked** |
| `kb_unset` (no SameSite) | not sent (Lax by default) | **sent** (None by default) | blocked |
| `kb_lax` | not sent | not sent | blocked |
| `kb_part` (`Partitioned`, None) | sent | sent | sent (the only one) |

## When cookies are sent

All three agree with the credentials rule mimicry already computes: CORS
requests without credentials (cross-origin `fetch()` and XHR by default,
`crossorigin=anonymous` elements, `credentials: "omit"`) send none; no-cors
requests, frames, beacons and `credentials: "include"` send them. Safari's
fonts load no-cors, so its cross-site fonts carry `kb_part` (once varying:
`font-css.cs` sent it in some samples only).

Redirect back to the page's site (`fetch-redirect.cs-so.hop`,
`image-redirect.cs-so.hop`):

| Chrome | Firefox | Safari |
|---|---|---|
| every first-party cookie | all but `k_lax` and `k_strict`: SameSite judged on the whole chain | **none at all**, `SameSite=None` included (tracking prevention, [inference]) |

## Order

- Chrome and Firefox: creation order (every cookie here has path `/`).
- Safari: `k_site` (the domain cookie) first, then the host cookies by
  name. The `set-cookie` scenario, where every cookie shares a domain, only
  showed "by name". Whether Safari sorts by domain then name, or by
  specificity, needs mixed domains and paths.

## The `cookies` scenario

Captured 2026-10-24, 3 samples per browser, no sample differing. Page A
(`www.kinds-a.test`) sets cookies, loads a third-party setter on
`cdn.kinds-b.test`, follows image redirects, navigates to a second
top-level site C (`www.kinds-c.test`), which loads images from the
cross-site host and from A, then navigates back to A by a link and by a
form POST.

| | Chrome | Firefox | Safari |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cross-site link (GET) to A | Lax, None, unset sent; Strict withheld | same | same |
| Cross-site form POST to A | Lax, Strict withheld; unset **sent** (seconds old: the two-minute exception for unset cookies, which Chrome otherwise treats as Lax) | unset sent (None by default) | unset sent (None by default) |
| `SameSite=None` without `Secure` | rejected | rejected | **kept and sent** |
| Cross-site host's cookies, requested under top-level site C | `cb_none` sent, `cb_part` withheld (partitioned by top-level site) | **none**: third-party cookies live in a jar per top-level site (Total Cookie Protection), so A's third-party cookies are not C's | none |
| A's own cookies, requested under top-level site C | `c_none` only | none (A's first-party jar is not used under C) | none |
| Redirect same-site → A | everything | everything | everything |
| Redirect cross-site → A | everything (redirects do not count) | Lax, Strict dropped (SameSite judged on the whole chain) | **none** |
| Redirect cross-site → same-site host | its domain cookies | its domain cookies | **none** |

Safari's rule is therefore: no cookies at all on a request reached through
a cross-site hop, whatever the target.

Order (`c-order.so`, paths `/` and `/k`, host-only and `Domain` cookies):

- Chrome, Firefox: `co_k co_dk | c_strict c_lax c_none c_unset co_dom
  co_alpha`: longer path first, then creation order (RFC 6265).
- Safari: `co_dk co_k | co_dom c_lax c_none c_noneinsecure c_strict c_unset
  co_alpha`: longer path first, then `Domain` cookies before host-only ones,
  then by name. For cookies matching one request, that is the same as
  sorting by stored domain (`.kinds-a.test` before `www.kinds-a.test`);
  two levels of `Domain` cookie would tell the two apart, uncaptured.
